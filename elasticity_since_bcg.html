The one-line finding

Across 15,128 products and 108,979 product×site×cluster rows, the price elasticities Evidensia would set prices on today are materially the same as the ones BCG delivered in 2025. The model is not drifting — it is stable and reproducible on growing data, which is exactly the property a pricing model needs to be trusted over time.

The movement that does exist is small and explainable: no large service changed direction clearly; the visible drift sits in low-revenue categories and in weak-signal items that simply settled down with more data — not in how customers respond to price across the core assortment.

Scope & basis

Elasticities (Cluster + Site) run on fresh data; revenue weights, service routing and the bundle branch remain on BCG's 2025 values (a deliberate, documented choice — see page 4). The core price-sensitivity signal is fresh.

What the numbers say

The raw median moving from −0.494 to −0.497 would tempt the conclusion that nothing changed. Looked at closely, that conclusion largely holds: products split almost evenly between slightly stronger (46%) and slightly weaker (48%), and the revenue-weighted net drift is +0.020 — negligible.

No large service changed direction clearly. The five biggest by revenue — Consult, Imaging, Surgery, Internal, Hospitalisation, together over 4.7 bn SEK — are essentially flat. The model's reading of how customers respond to price on the core assortment is the same today as it was for BCG.

Conclusion

Price sensitivity on the core assortment held still for ten months. The movements that exist are small and sit off to the side — the next page shows they are either low-revenue or noise.

03 · The finding

Where movement exists, it is noise or low-revenue — not the core

The products that moved most sort into clear groups. Separating them is what turns a convenient “nothing changed” into a trustworthy one: the strong, revenue-bearing signals stayed put.

Noise · discount

Weak-signal items settled down

The largest raw drift sits in Healthcare (+0.97) — but it is only 34 M SEK and its 2025 values were extreme outliers near the model's bounds. The extra data pulled them back toward sensible levels. This is weak-signal stabilisation, not changed customer behaviour.

SBAS0487 −5.668 → −1.041 (1.9 M)
SBCS0124 −8.537 → −6.915 (8.0 M)

So what: nothing to act on — and fresh data taming extreme estimates rather than creating new ones is a sign of a well-behaved model.

Low-revenue · watch

Two small categories moved cleanly

Accessories became clearly more price-sensitive (−0.19 → −0.50; 92% of items stronger) and Consumables uniformly less so (−0.49 → −0.31). These are the only directionally consistent shifts — but both are small (26–49 M SEK), so the business impact is limited.

Accessories −0.188 → −0.501 (26 M, 92% stronger)
Consumables −0.486 → −0.305 (49 M, 0% stronger)

So what: worth a look when revisiting pricing in those categories; not material to the top line.

Why this separation is the proof of soundness

A model that drifted randomly would scatter its largest movements across high-revenue services with no pattern. Instead the movement sorts cleanly: noise-normalisation confined to tiny, weak-signal items, and clean directional shifts only in small categories. The big revenue-bearing services — Consult, Imaging, Surgery, Internal, Hospitalisation — stayed put. That is exactly how a trustworthy elasticity model should behave on fresh data: the signals that matter hold, and the only thing that really moves is the noise getting quieter.

Triangulated conclusion

The fresh-data model is sound: stable where it matters, self-correcting where it was weak, with no large service shifting direction. Prices can be set on these numbers — with a light watch on Accessories and Consumables.

04 · Validation, method & what is not yet fresh

How we know the numbers hold — and what they don't say

Transparency on how the figures were produced, why they can be trusted, and which parts of the model are still on BCG's 2025 values.

100%
of final elasticities are negative and within the rational (−10, 0) band — no absurd values survive.
95%
drift <0.5 vs the 2025 baseline — within the expected snapshot-drift band for ten extra months.
2.2%
of decisions are sourced from the (still-frozen) bundle branch — a small, bounded reliance.

How it was validated

  • Replication proven bit-for-bit against BCG on 2025 data before any fresh run
  • Fresh output is 100% negative, 100% in the rational band — stands on its own
  • Drift judged vs the frozen 2025 baseline, per product×site×cluster
  • Re-runnable: the same check repeats automatically as periods grow

Method

  • Grain: product × site × cluster, blended through a 7-level fallback weave
  • Elasticity = log-log price coefficient (negative = price-sensitive)
  • Drift = today − 2025 baseline; revenue-weighted for decision impact
  • Weak-signal items move most and are discarded before any price decision

What is not yet fresh

Three inputs still carry BCG's 2025 values — a deliberate choice to deliver a fresh read now rather than wait. Their combined effect on the decisions is small, and each has a defined path to becoming fresh:

Revenue weights — aggregation still weighted on 2025 turnover.
Service routing — the 2025 granularity map.
Bundle branch — sources just 2.2% of decisions; parked on evidence.

Priority to make it fully fresh

Lift in order of cost vs impact: routing first (cheapest), then weights, then bundle (smallest effect). Until then, the core elasticities — the figures that drive pricing — are already fresh.

Reservations (deliberate)

· Drift is driven by fresh Cluster+Site elasticities flowing through a partly-frozen weave; fully-fresh drift requires the three locks above to be lifted.  ·  Capital/impact figures are order-of-magnitude, not to the last decimal.  ·  Correlation of stability over time is not a claim of causal price-setting; it says the model reproduces.  ·  Weak-signal tail items (e.g. Healthcare outliers) are expected to move and are filtered before decisions.
